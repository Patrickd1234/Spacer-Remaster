# Spacer wins by owning the company layer

**Spacer is not a weaker Jira or a smaller monday.com. It is a different product, and its best chance is to lean into that.** It already matches Jira's Scrum basics (sprints, story points, burndown, velocity, retros, custom Kanban columns). It also bundles a "company layer" that neither rival offers natively: announcements, a company calendar, a directory with reporting lines, department stand-ups and chat. All of it sits on database-level permissions and MFA that are stronger than most MVPs have. The weaknesses are just as clear. Spacer has none of the platform features buyers expect: automations, integrations, custom fields, timeline views, mobile apps, AI and import/export. More urgently, several screens show fake or non-working controls, task comments have a table but no UI, search is a three-result substring match, and the layout is desktop-only. That last point matters in a market where data costs **₦575/GB**. Upwork is a freelance marketplace, not a competitor. Three of its ideas transfer well: an explicit "submit for review" step with an auto-accept deadline, guest access limited to one project, and AI summaries of work evidence. Its screenshot and keystroke tracking should be refused outright. My recommendation is to position Spacer as the operating space for multi-department small and mid-size companies in Nigeria, priced flat in naira. Win on coherence, speed and trust, not on configurability. The order of work is: remove the placeholders, finish the collaboration core and search, make it work on phones, add cross-department and guest work, get commercially ready, and only then build automations and AI.

*How to read this report: facts about Spacer come from a read of the codebase on 19 Nov 2026 and link to local files. Facts about Jira, monday.com, Upwork and the market link to web sources. Paragraphs or table rows marked **Claude's judgement** are my own opinions and inferences, not findings from the sources.*

## Spacer already matches Jira's Scrum core and beats both rivals on the company layer

Spacer's Scrum features are not a toy. Real sprints replaced an earlier browser-only timer. They have a committed baseline, burndown, velocity, retrospectives, a backlog, planned sprints and carry-over, and only project managers can run them ([DEVELOPMENT_LOG.md](../DEVELOPMENT_LOG.md)). Boards support per-project columns and drag-and-drop ([SECURITY_AND_FIXES_LOG.md](../Spacer-app/docs/SECURITY_AND_FIXES_LOG.md)). That covers what Jira's Scrum and Kanban boards are mainly bought for. In monday.com's portfolio, sprints, burndown and velocity live in the separate monday dev product ([monday dev](https://monday.com/dev)). The comparison is about the dev department, though. G2's editor says monday.com is best for non-technical departments and "often" loses to Jira for engineering-heavy teams ([G2 Learn](https://learn.g2.com/monday-review)). Spacer gives each department its own space and gives the dev department real sprints, in one product.

The second strength is the one competitors leave to other tools. Spacer has pinned announcements with cover images and a role-scoped directory showing who reports to whom. It also has stand-ups per department, DMs with reactions and attachments, and group and project channels ([DEVELOPMENT_LOG.md](../DEVELOPMENT_LOG.md), [GroupChat.jsx](../Spacer-app/src/components/GroupChat.jsx)). Jira and monday.com expect Slack, Confluence or an HR system to do this. The market data favours consolidation. Atlassian's own survey of 12,000 knowledge workers found teams **waste 25% of their time searching for answers** ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/state-of-teams-2025)), and the average company now runs **101 apps** ([Stacker, citing Okta](https://stacker.com/stories/business-economy/toggle-tax-what-1200-daily-app-switches-are-costing-sales-teams)). The brand copy already makes the right promise: "One workspace for every company. HR, Design, Dev and Marketing — each in their own space" ([Login.jsx](../Spacer-app/src/Login.jsx)).

The third strength is security engineering, and buyers now rank security first: **71% rate it critical** and 39% say security concerns triggered their latest purchase ([Business Wire / Capterra](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250904859415/en/AI-and-Security-Drive-Project-Management-Software-Investment-Capterra-Survey-Finds)). Row-level security is on for every table in the schema snapshot (33 tables, 133 policies). A restrictive policy means a token that hasn't passed MFA can't read data once a user has enrolled a second factor ([enforce_mfa migration](../Spacer-app/supabase/migrations/20261018090000_enforce_mfa.sql)). RBAC uses six fixed roles, 23 permissions and 73 grants, checked in the database. It was validated against the old model with **11,304 compared answers and no unexpected differences** ([RBAC.md](../Spacer-app/docs/RBAC.md), [DEVELOPMENT_LOG.md](../DEVELOPMENT_LOG.md)). Fixed roles are also a usability advantage. Complex permission schemes that "need admin expertise" are a standing Jira complaint ([G2](https://g2.com/products/jira/reviews?page=12)), while Jira's Free plan lacks project-level permissions entirely ([resolution.de](https://www.resolution.de/post/jira-pricing-plans/)).

**Claude's judgement:** the table shows why a feature-for-feature race is unwinnable. Atlassian reported **$6.57B** in FY26 revenue ([Atlassian Q4 FY26](https://s206.q4cdn.com/270053503/files/doc_financials/2026/q4/TEAM-Q4-2026-Earnings-Release.pdf)), and monday.com is rebuilding itself around AI agents ([IT Brief](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/monday-com-rebuilds-platform-around-ai-work-agents)). Spacer's top three rows are things the giants can't easily copy without changing their business: one coherent company workspace, real sprints for the dev team, and simple, strong permissions. The bottom rows are gaps to close only as far as customers need, not to parity.

## Placeholders, missing comments and a desktop-only layout undercut the trust story

The most damaging gap is not a missing feature but fake ones. Settings shows **"14 of 25 seats used"** with a 56% bar, and both are hard-coded. The "Automated Domain Joining" switch only changes local state. The "Weekly Productivity Digest", "Browser Desktop Push Notifications" and "Notification Delivery Frequency" toggles are saved but do nothing, and there is no service worker ([Settings.jsx](../Spacer-app/src/Settings.jsx), [PreferencesContext.jsx](../Spacer-app/src/PreferencesContext.jsx)). Placeholders remain elsewhere too: the project page still says "Meeting creation functionality coming soon...", while the company calendar has a disabled "Connect Calendar (coming soon)" button and the login page has a disabled Google sign-in ([project_page.jsx](../Spacer-app/src/project_page.jsx), [CompanyCalendar.jsx](../Spacer-app/src/general/CompanyCalendar.jsx), [Login.jsx](../Spacer-app/src/Login.jsx)). **Claude's judgement:** a product whose strongest argument is trust can't show invented numbers on its admin screen. One admin who notices the seat count is wrong will doubt the permission model too. Removing or honestly labelling these items is the cheapest high-value work available.

The second gap is the collaboration core inside a task. There is a `comments` table, an `attachments` table and a `milestones` table, but no `src/` code queries any of them. Tasks have one assignee, no labels, no dependencies and no start date, and subtasks are stored as JSON text that has already caused parsing bugs ([schema.sql](../Spacer-app/supabase/schema/schema.sql), [DEVELOPMENT_LOG.md](../DEVELOPMENT_LOG.md)). monday.com's highest-rated features are basics: task creation and assignment (94%), due dates (94%) and prioritisation (93%) ([G2 Learn](https://learn.g2.com/monday-review)). Without comments, discussion about a task leaves the task and goes into chat, where it can't be found later. That works against the consolidation pitch.

Search makes this worse. The header runs a substring `ilike` match on task titles, project names and user names, limited to **three results each**. A full-text `search` edge function that respects each caller's permissions already exists, and nothing calls it ([header.jsx](../Spacer-app/src/partials/header.jsx), [search/index.ts](../Spacer-app/supabase/functions/search/index.ts)). If Spacer's pitch is "stop losing a quarter of your time hunting across tools", search across tasks, messages, announcements and files is the feature that proves it.

Mobile is the third gap, and the Nigerian market sharpens it. The sidebar is a fixed 260px column. About 20,000 lines of JSX contain only 56 `sm:` breakpoints, and there is no mobile menu, PWA manifest or native app ([WorkspaceSidebar.jsx](../Spacer-app/src/partials/WorkspaceSidebar.jsx)). Jira and monday.com both include iOS and Android apps on their free plans ([resolution.de](https://www.resolution.de/post/jira-pricing-plans/), [monday.com pricing](https://monday.com/pricing)). In Nigeria the median mobile download speed is **44 Mbps** ([BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/technology/article/nigeria-slips-to-85th-in-global-internet-speed-rankings-as-peers-pull-ahead/)), and the average data price doubled to **about ₦575/GB in 2025** ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2026/02/09/how-nigerians-spent-%E2%82%A620-87billion-daily-on-data-in-2025/)). So a responsive, data-frugal web app is a selling point, not just a convenience.

The remaining gaps are engineering reliability and the multi-department promise itself. All **147 tests pass**, but they cover only pure logic. There are no component, integration or end-to-end tests, and the development log often notes that a change was "Not checked in a browser". Several files are well over the project's own 800-line limit (`project_page.jsx` is **2,131 lines**), and a lot of work is uncommitted, including the meetings and task-key migrations, which aren't yet on the live database ([DEVELOPMENT_LOG.md](../DEVELOPMENT_LOG.md), [package.json](../Spacer-app/package.json)). Smaller security loose ends remain. The `users` table is still readable across the whole organization even though the Directory page hides some people. Reports reads `userId` from localStorage. The Vercel config sets no Content-Security-Policy ([DEVELOPMENT_LOG.md](../DEVELOPMENT_LOG.md), [Reports.jsx](../Spacer-app/src/Reports.jsx), [vercel.json](../Spacer-app/vercel.json)). Most strategically, collaboration on a project shared by several departments is logged as **"not built anywhere yet"** ([DEVELOPMENT_LOG.md](../DEVELOPMENT_LOG.md)). **Claude's judgement:** this is the biggest gap in the product's own story. A tool sold as "every department in one place" needs a marketing-and-dev launch, or an HR-and-finance onboarding, to work as one project.

## Upwork contributes three ideas worth taking, and one to refuse

On Upwork, work is managed through a contract, not a task. Fixed-price work is split into milestones. The freelancer presses **"Submit work"**, which starts a **14-day review clock**. The client approves or requests changes, and if the client does nothing the funds release automatically ([Upwork support](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/17974824831507), [AIProposer](https://aiproposer.com/guides/upwork-strategy/upwork-contract-milestones)). Each contract has a workroom holding its messages, files, calls and recent files ([Upwork support](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/17975182774163--Use-the-contract-workroom)). Enterprise accounts add team-level approval rules with sequential or simultaneous approvers ([Upwork blog](https://www.upwork.com/blog/advancing-enterprise-solutions)). In 2026 Upwork's AI work focused on **Work Diary summaries, meeting recaps and contract drafting** ([Upwork Spring 2026](https://www.upwork.com/press/releases/upwork-updates-spring-2026-ai-powered-innovations-to-help-small-businesses-get-ambitious-work-done)).

**Claude's judgement:** three of these transfer directly. The first is a review state for deliverables: a task or milestone moves to *Submitted*, then to *Approved* or *Changes requested*, with a deadline after which it escalates or is auto-accepted. Only an explicit "Submit for review" action starts the clock. This suits non-dev departments (design sign-off, HR document approval, marketing copy) far better than "In Review" columns, and it gives the unused `milestones` table a purpose. The second is guest access scoped to one project, the way a freelancer sees only their own workroom. Spacer has no external user type today, only an internal Viewer role ([schema.sql](../Spacer-app/supabase/schema/schema.sql)), and agencies or companies with contractors will ask for it early. monday.com sells guest access from its Standard tier ([monday.com pricing](https://monday.com/pricing)). The third is summaries of work evidence: a weekly "what happened on this project" digest built from `activity_log`, stand-ups and meeting notes. Spacer's project page, with its Messages, Files, Calendar and Recent Activity tabs, is already a workroom. Only the summary layer is missing.

The idea to refuse is Upwork's proof-of-work machinery. Its tracker takes **screenshots about six times an hour** and counts keystrokes and clicks ([Upwork support](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/211064098-Log-Time-with-Time-Tracker)), and freelancers have complained about it for years even though they are paid per hour by strangers ([Upwork Community](https://community.upwork.com/t5/Freelancers/Desktop-App-Can-you-turn-off-the-screenshot-function/m-p/806277)). **Claude's judgement:** inside a company the same features read as surveillance. They would also create new personal-data processing under Nigeria's data protection law. Proof should come from outcomes: a linked commit or merged PR (which is exactly what the in-progress GitHub automation provides), an attached file or a reviewer's approval. Escrow, fees, proposals and public ratings are marketplace mechanics and don't apply.

## Price in naira, sell to the whole company, skip the configuration race

Both incumbents are making pricing harder for small buyers. After the 13 October 2026 increase, Jira Standard costs **$9.70/user/month** for the first 100 users on monthly billing ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/licensing/future-pricing/cloud/list/faqs)). From 3 December 2026, automation and Rovo AI usage beyond the included allowance is billed, with overage switched on by default ([Breeze](https://www.breeze.pm/articles/atlassian-pricing-changes-2026)). monday.com charges $9/$12/$19 per seat annually, with a **3-seat minimum and seat buckets, so a team of six pays for ten** ([monday.com pricing](https://monday.com/pricing), [TinyCommand](https://tinycommand.com/blogs/monday-pricing-explained)). Standard allows **250 automation actions a month** for the whole account. Capterra reviewers rate its pricing structure 54% negative ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/147657/monday-com/reviews/?page=17)). monday.com's growth is coming from large accounts: customers paying over $50K grew 26%, while accounts with more than 10 users grew only 5% ([SEC 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001845338/000117891326005085/exhibit_99-1.htm)). In Nigeria the dollar price is only part of the problem. Some banks cap international card spending at **about $20 a month** ([TechPoint Africa](https://techpoint.africa/insight/nigerian-virtual-dollar-cards/)), while Paystack and Flutterwave bill in naira ([Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/accept-payments-africa)). Across SaaS, pure seat pricing fell from 21% to 15% of companies in a year ([Enterprise DNA](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/ai-pulse/ai-pulse-2026-07-26-per-seat-saas-pricing-keeps-eroding-as-ai-agents-don-t-hold)). Basecamp shows flat pricing can work at about **$299/month for unlimited users** ([Orb](https://www.withorb.com/pricing-atlas/basecamp)).

The history of challengers supports a focused, opinionated product. Linear won with "one really good way of doing things", speed, and word of mouth, and it was profitable by 2021 ([Aakash Gupta](https://www.news.aakashg.com/p/how-linear-grows)). Jira's new-company adoptions fell **32%** year on year while Linear's rose **67%** ([Bloomberry](https://bloomberry.com/blog/the-data-on-jira-vs-linear-whos-winning-new-customers/)). ClickUp's "everything" approach reached about $300M ARR but is restructuring after cutting 22% of staff ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/clickup-layoffs-22-percent-ai-100x-org-million-salary)). Height, an AI-first tracker, shut down in September 2025 ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/3/height-project-management-tool-to-shut-down-by-september-2025)). AI is now the top purchase trigger for 55% of buyers ([Business Wire / Capterra](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250904859415/en/AI-and-Security-Drive-Project-Management-Software-Investment-Capterra-Survey-Finds)), but **62–64% of workers call AI agents unreliable**. What they most want help with is mundane: meeting notes (43%), organising documents (31%) and scheduling (27%) ([UNLEASH](https://www.unleash.ai/artificial-intelligence/asana-64-of-employees-believe-ai-agents-are-unreliable-calling-for-more-training-clarity-and-guardrails/), [Techstrong.ai](https://techstrong.ai/agentic-ai/workers-want-to-hand-over-a-third-of-their-jobs-to-ai-but-trust-remains-elusive/)).

**Claude's judgement on positioning:** Spacer should present itself as *the operating space for a growing company*. That means structure on the level of Jira's for the dev department, simplicity on the level of monday.com's for everyone else, and the company layer (announcements, directory, stand-ups, chat) that neither provides. The first target is Nigerian and West African firms with roughly 20–200 staff and three or more departments. Price per organization or per department in naira, billed through Paystack or Flutterwave, with no seat minimums or buckets and with automations included rather than metered. There is a case for an alternative: go dev-first like Linear, since the dev department is Spacer's most developed use case and GitHub automation is underway. I'd reject it. That market is where Jira is now assigning work to coding agents ([Futurum](https://futurumgroup.com/?p=93113)) and where Linear already owns speed. Spacer's real differentiation only shows when HR and marketing are in the same workspace. Treat the dev team as the showcase inside each customer, not the whole market. Two cautions apply. The notes contain **no data on Nigerian companies' willingness to pay** for project tools, and the repo states no target size or pricing. So the first job of a pricing page is to test those assumptions with design-partner customers. Separately, GAID 2025 requires transfer impact assessments for cross-border data ([DLA Piper](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2025/06/nigeria-ndpc-issues-gaid-key-compliance-insights/)). Spacer should publish where its Supabase data is hosted (this was not checked) and offer a data processing agreement. That turns a compliance duty into a local advantage that foreign vendors rarely address.

What not to build is part of the positioning. **Claude's judgement:** skip a Jira-style workflow engine with conditions and validators. Skip a monday-style custom-column builder, an app marketplace, screenshot time tracking and autonomous AI agents. Each adds the complexity that drives users away from the incumbents: Jira's "steep learning curve" draws hundreds of G2 mentions ([G2](https://g2.com/products/jira/reviews?page=4)), and monday.com has **503 "Overwhelming Features" tags** ([G2](https://www.g2.com/survey_responses/monday-ai-work-platform-review-3603798)). A short list of labels and a handful of fixed automation rules cover most of the need. Templates by department are worth building, because Atlassian is now spending AI effort precisely on setup burden with Rovo-generated spaces ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/development/jira-summer-release)).

## Six steps, cheapest credibility fixes first

The whole sequence below is **Claude's judgement**. The "Evidence" column names the finding that motivates each step. GitHub commit automation is already planned and its first step (task keys) is built. It runs in parallel and is covered in [GitHub commit task automation.md](GitHub%20commit%20task%20automation.md).

| Step | Work | Why now | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Honesty pass (days) | Remove the hard-coded seat counter. Hide or label the non-working toggles and "coming soon" buttons. Apply and commit the meetings and task-key migrations. Use one brand name ("Spacer" vs "Spacers"). Replace the Vite README. Stop reading identity from localStorage. | Cheapest work with the highest trust payoff | Settings.jsx, project_page.jsx, Reports.jsx, git status |
| 2. Collaboration core | Task comments with @mentions and task attachments, using the existing tables. Wire the full-text search function into the header and extend it to messages and announcements. Make the weekly digest real, giving people control over notification volume. | Keeps discussion on the task and proves the "find anything" promise | Unused `comments`/`attachments` tables; Atlassian's 25% search figure; monday.com notification-noise complaints |
| 3. Phones and reliability | Responsive layout with a collapsible sidebar, then a PWA with data-frugal loading. Playwright tests for signup → launch → invite, task create/move and the sprint flow. Split files over 800 lines. | Most users will open it on a phone; UI is the least-verified layer | Fixed 260px sidebar; ₦575/GB; 147 logic-only tests |
| 4. The multi-department promise | Projects shared across departments. Guest access scoped to one project. A submit-for-review/approve state with a deadline, using milestones. Department templates (HR onboarding, campaign launch, sprint project). | This is the product's stated reason to exist | "Not built anywhere yet"; Upwork's review flow and workroom; monday.com guest access on Standard |
| 5. Commercial readiness | Landing and pricing page. Naira billing via Paystack/Flutterwave with real seat counts. NDPA/GAID page and DPA. CSV export of reports. A Jira/Trello/CSV importer. A CSP header. | Lets the founder charge, and lets switchers bring their data | Card limits; GAID transfer rules; importers as a growth lever ([Shortcut](https://www.shortcut.com/import/height)) |
| 6. Platform layer | Finish the GitHub automation phases. Add Google Calendar sync and Slack notifications. Add a few fixed automation rules (e.g. "when all subtasks are done, move the task to Review"). Add opt-in AI summaries: weekly project digest, stand-up rollup, meeting recap to action items. | AI needs the comment, meeting and activity data from steps 2–4 to be useful | Workers want meeting notes and summaries; agents are seen as unreliable |

Design-partner pilots can start after step 2 without billing. Steps 3 and 4 can overlap if there are two developers. **Claude's judgement:** I'd keep step 6 late even though AI drives purchases. Every competitor already has AI, so Spacer gains nothing by shipping a thinner version early. AI summaries built on a workspace that holds the tasks, chat, stand-ups and meetings would be better than what a single-purpose tool can produce, but only once that data actually exists in Spacer.

## Conclusion

Spacer's main risk isn't any one missing feature. It is that the product looks further along than it is. Fake seat counts and dead toggles sit next to genuinely strong database security, and buyers who rank security first will judge the whole product by its weakest visible screen. The flip side is good news: the most valuable next work is small, and much of it reuses tables and edge functions that already exist but aren't connected to the UI.

The incumbents' strategies leave Spacer a real opening. Jira is moving toward AI agents and metered usage, and monday.com is moving upmarket with credit-based AI pricing. Neither is aiming at a 40-person Lagos company that wants HR, marketing and dev in one affordable, fast, trustworthy place, paid for in naira. Spacer should judge its progress by whether whole companies adopt it, not by how many features it matches. A useful test is whether a non-technical department head in a customer company uses Spacer every day without being told to.
